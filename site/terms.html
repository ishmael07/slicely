2. The service

Slicely does three things:

  • Finds. It searches public 3D model sites for a model matching what you asked for, and resolves links and repositories you paste.
  • Slices. It chooses an orientation and slicing settings from the model's geometry and your stated goal, and runs PrusaSlicer to produce G-code.
  • Sends. It uploads that G-code to a printer you have connected, or gives you the file.

Slicely is not a CAD tool and does not create models. It works only with models that already exist — ones it finds, ones you link to, and ones you upload. Nothing it produces is an original design of ours.

The service is provided as-is. It is not certified, approved, or warranted for any particular purpose, and in particular it is not suitable for producing medical, structural, load-bearing, safety-critical, aerospace, automotive, or life-support parts.

4. 3D printing safety

You are solely responsible for your printer and everything it does. That includes the machine itself, the materials you load, the ventilation of the room, the clearance of the build plate, the condition of the bed surface, the firmware, and the supervision of every print from start to finish.

An FDM printer is a fire risk. It holds a heater above 200 °C, often unattended, frequently overnight. Heater failures, thermal runaway, loose wiring, failed thermistors, and blobs of molten plastic engulfing a hotend are all documented, ordinary failure modes of consumer printers. Do not run a printer unattended, in a space without a working smoke alarm, near anything flammable, or in a room where people sleep.

Slicely never starts a print on its own. Sending a file uploads it and queues it. A print begins only if you have separately armed auto-start for that specific printer, in settings, yourself — the AI agent cannot arm it and cannot start a print you have not armed. If you do arm auto-start, you accept that a print may begin on a bed you have not checked, and that starting a print on a plate that still holds the previous part can ruin the print and damage the machine. No consumer FDM printer reliably senses a clear bed. Checking the bed is your job.

Slice settings are suggestions, and they can be wrong. Orientation scores, support decisions, time and filament estimates, layer heights, and temperature choices are produced by heuristics and by a language model. They are not a simulation and carry no guarantee of correctness, printability, dimensional accuracy, or strength. Review the preview and the settings before you print, and do not rely on a Slicely estimate for anything that matters.

You are responsible for the legality and safety of what you choose to print, and for complying with the handling and safety instructions of your printer and your filament.
