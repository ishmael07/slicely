Privacy Policy

Effective September 14, 2026

Slicely is operated by Ishmael Lallmahamad, an individual developer ("we", "us").

Slicely has no accounts, no analytics and no advertising, so there is not much to say here — but what there is, is said plainly. There are two ways to run Slicely and they differ completely: the hosted web app, where your data sits on our server, and the macOS app, where it never leaves your Mac. Where the two differ, this policy says so.

1. What we collect

  • A session cookie. One functional cookie holding an opaque session id. It is what keeps your chats and settings attached to you. It is not a tracking cookie and is not shared with anyone.
  • Your chats. What you type and what the agent replies, including the tool calls it made along the way.
  • Files you bring. Models you upload, models fetched from a link you pasted, and models downloaded from a source site on your behalf.
  • Sliced output. The G-code and the metrics produced from those files.
  • Printer connection details, including the address of each printer and the credentials or API keys it needs. Credentials are stored encrypted.
  • Your AI provider credentials (your Anthropic or OpenAI API key), stored encrypted at rest. They are never logged, never returned in any response, and never included in an error message; the app displays only a hint of the key's last few characters.
  • Server logs containing IP addresses, timestamps, and request paths, kept for 14 days to debug failures and to catch abuse.

If you sign in. Signing in with Google or GitHub is optional — it is what grants the one-time free AI credit — and it stores a small record: the email address the provider confirms, the display name it returns, and the single letter we draw in the header from that name. We keep no provider tokens: the access token from the sign-in is used once to read those fields and is never written down, so we cannot act on your Google or GitHub account and have nothing to refresh. To stop one address claiming the free credit over and over, we record a one-way hash of your IP address for the day you sign up (the address itself is never stored, and the hash cannot be turned back into it). Every model call your free credit pays for appends one line to a usage ledger — the model, the token counts and the fraction of a cent it cost — which is how the balance in the header is worked out. Delete my data removes all of it: the account record, the balance, the ledger lines, and the chats, files and keys in your session. The email address is then retired rather than reused, so the free credit is not granted twice.

2. What we do not collect

No analytics. No Google Analytics, no product analytics, no session recording, no heatmaps, no third-party scripts of any kind. The landing page loads no external resources at all — even the font is served from our own domain.

No advertising and no tracking. We run no ads, set no advertising or tracking cookies, and share nothing with data brokers or ad networks.

No password. Signing in is optional and goes through Google or GitHub, so we never see, set, or store a password — and you can use Slicely with your own API key and no account at all.

No training. We do not use your chats, files, or prints to train any model.

3. Where it lives

Hosted web app: on our server's encrypted volume, in the region reported by the app's own /api/config endpoint. Your session's files live in a workspace that only your session can address; sliced output is reachable only via an opaque token, never a file path.

macOS app: entirely on your Mac, under your user account. Nothing is sent to us at all — we do not operate a service the desktop app talks to.

4. Who receives it

  • Anthropic or OpenAI. Your prompts, and text derived from your files (names, dimensions, geometry summaries, slice metrics), are sent to the API of the provider you chose to run the agent. With your own key that happens under your own agreement with that provider; on free credit it happens under ours, and the provider still receives nothing beyond the prompt itself.
  • Google or GitHub, if you sign in. They learn that you signed in to Slicely and tell us your confirmed email address and display name; we send them nothing else.
  • The model sites you search. A search sends your query to the sources it covers — Thingiverse, Printables, MyMiniFactory, NIH 3D, Smithsonian, NASA, GitHub, MakerWorld and others — and a download fetches from whichever site hosts the file. Those sites see the request and their own privacy policies apply to it.
  • Your printer, or your cloud print service. Sending a file transmits the G-code, and the credentials you configured, to the printer or service you chose.
  • Our hosting provider, which operates the server and stores the encrypted volume on our behalf.
  • No one else. We do not sell, rent, or share your data, and there is no advertising or analytics recipient of any kind. We would disclose data if legally compelled, and would tell you unless prohibited from doing so.

5. How long we keep it

In the hosted web app:

  • Sessions — including chats, settings, printers, and your encrypted key — expire after 30 days idle, and everything in them is deleted.
  • Workspace files — uploads, downloaded models, and sliced output — are swept after 2 hours idle, well before the session itself expires.
  • Server logs are kept 14 days.
  • "Delete my data" in the app removes your session, your chats, your files, your printer credentials, your key and — if you signed in — your account record, balance and usage ledger immediately, without waiting for any of the above.

In the macOS app, nothing expires and nothing is swept by us; the files are yours to keep or delete.

6. Your rights

Everything we hold about you is visible in the app and deletable from it. Use "Delete my data" to erase all of it at once, or write to ishmaellallmahamad2@gmail.com and we will help. If you never signed in there is no account to look up, so tell us from the browser session concerned, or the request may be impossible to fulfil.

GDPR and UK GDPR. If you are in the EEA or the UK, our lawful basis for processing is the performance of the contract you enter into by using Slicely (Art. 6(1)(b)), and our legitimate interest in keeping the service secure and working for the server logs (Art. 6(1)(f)). You have the right to access, rectify, erase, restrict, and port your data, and to object to processing; the controller is Ishmael Lallmahamad, an individual developer. You may complain to your local supervisory authority. We do not make automated decisions producing legal effects about you.

CCPA / CPRA. If you are a California resident: we do not sell or share your personal information as those terms are defined, and we have not done so in the preceding 12 months. You have the right to know, to delete, and to correct, and you will never be discriminated against for exercising them.

7. Children

Slicely is not intended for anyone under 16, and we do not knowingly collect data from children. If you believe a child has used Slicely, contact ishmaellallmahamad2@gmail.com and we will delete the session.

8. Changes

We will update this policy when what the software actually does changes. The effective date at the top moves with it, and the full history is public in the Slicely repository. Material changes will be announced in the app.
