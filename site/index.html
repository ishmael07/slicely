How it works

Four steps, one conversation.

1

Connect your key

Paste an Anthropic API key once. It is encrypted at rest and only ever sent to Anthropic.

2

Say what you want

Type it, paste a link to a model page or a repo, or drop in your own STL · 3MF · OBJ · AMF · STEP.

3

Slicely finds, orients, slices

It searches every source, scores the candidate poses, and derives the settings from the model's real geometry.

4

Send it to your printer

Upload straight to OctoPrint, Klipper, PrusaLink, Prusa Connect, Bambu over LAN, or a file for the SD card.

◆ Prints never start on their own. You arm that per printer, and only you can.

What it actually does

Find, slice, send. Slicely is not a CAD tool — it works with models that already exist, plus whatever you bring it.

Eight sources at once

One query across Thingiverse, Printables, MyMiniFactory, NIH 3D, Smithsonian, NASA, GitHub and MakerWorld, ranked together — and one of them being down never blanks your results. Three more (Thangs, Yeggi, STLFinder) sit behind bot protection that blocks honestly-identified requests, so they ship switched off.

Paste any link

A model page, a raw .stl, a .zip of parts or a GitHub repo — Slicely resolves it, finds the meshes and downloads them.

Upload your own

Drag in STL, 3MF, OBJ, AMF or STEP — or a ZIP of parts that Slicely unpacks for you.

Smart orientation

Candidate poses scored on overhang area, bed contact and layer count, weighted by your goal — then explained in plain language.

Slice for your goal

Layer height, infill, walls, solid layers, supports and brim derived from the real geometry plus fast, detail or strength — override anything and the rest adapts.

Big multi-part jobs

Dozens of parts planned as one job: oriented, grouped so each plate needs the fewest tool changes, packed across plates and sliced in order — and one plate failing doesn't stop the rest.

Multi-colour

Split one STL into the parts hiding inside it so each gets its own filament, or band it by height with a swap pause — which works on any printer, AMS or not.

Six ways to reach a printer

OctoPrint, Klipper/Moonraker, PrusaLink, Prusa Connect, Bambu over LAN, or a file for the SD card — with live state, temperatures and pause, resume, cancel.

Chat history

Streaming replies with a collapsible thought process, model cards and slice-metric panels inline — and conversations you can come back to.

Pick your model & effort

Choose which Claude model runs the agent and how hard it thinks; your choice sticks between sessions.

Your printer sits at 192.168.x.x behind your router: a hosted server can't route to it, and browsers can't open raw TCP or MQTT. Cloud-connected printers work from anywhere; LAN printers need Slicely running on your own network.

Bring your own AI

Slicely runs on your own Anthropic API key. Create a personal key — give it an expiry — at console.anthropic.com, paste it in once, and you're going. You pay Anthropic directly for what you use; we take no cut and never resell your usage.

Your key is encrypted at rest and sent only to Anthropic. It never appears in a page, a log, or an error message — the app only ever shows you the last few characters. Delete it whenever you like, from Settings here or from your Anthropic console.

A Claude Pro or Max subscription cannot be used here. Those cover claude.ai; the API is billed separately. There is no Slicely account, no password, and no email to give us.

What you need

On the web

A browser. That's it — no install, and nothing to keep updated.

On a Mac

PrusaSlicer installed, for the slicing itself. Searching and importing work without it.

Either way

An Anthropic API key of your own — and a printer, when you want to send something to one.
